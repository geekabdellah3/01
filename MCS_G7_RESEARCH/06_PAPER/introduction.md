# 1. Introduction

Management control research has long argued that controls operate as a *package* rather than in isolation (Malmi & Brown, 2008; Grabner & Moers, 2013), whereas economists measure management through scored practices and relate the aggregate to productivity (Bloom & Van Reenen, 2007). The two traditions raise a question that is rarely posed explicitly: when firms are observed deploying individual practices, do their combinations form qualitatively distinct configurations, or do they mainly reflect different levels of managerial sophistication?

We address this question exploratorily with the management module of the World Bank Enterprise Surveys (WBES) in seven G7 economies in 2024–2025. We do not test the full doctoral model on artificial intelligence, internal auditing or dynamic capabilities; WBES does not measure these constructs. Our aims are to (i) describe the empirical structure of observable control practices, (ii) test configurations against a continuous gradient, and (iii) propose a model, restricted to what is observable, that can inform subsequent questionnaire design.

Configurations of control have been investigated before (Bedford & Malmi, 2015), and latent class analysis has been applied to Enterprise Surveys data on transactional governance (Murrell, Karalashvili & Francis, 2024). Our contribution is therefore not the use of a configurational method itself but a likelihood-based comparison of discrete, continuous and hybrid structures on practice-level data from several advanced economies, with explicit attention to survey skip patterns and cross-country comparability.
