# 4. Data and measurement

**Sample.** The merged WBES file contains 10,968 establishments from Canada (2024), France (2025), Germany (2025), Italy (2024), Japan (2025), the United Kingdom (2024) and the United States (2024). The original country files with value labels were not available. The management-practices module is asked of 6,316 establishments (88.5% with 20 or more permanent full-time employees); the others are outside the module's universe (Table 1).

**Practices.** Monitoring is whether the establishment tracked production or service performance indicators (r2, yes = 1); Targets is whether it had production or service targets (r4); Incentives is whether bonuses were tied to production or service targets (r8). Their direction (yes = 1) is verified by the skip logic of the data (follow-up items are asked only after a yes). Verification status of the wording: r2 and r8 partly verified through secondary descriptions; r4 inferred from its follow-up item r5, whose label (time frame of production or service targets) is verified for Italy 2024; r3 (number of indicators, asked only if r2 = yes) partly verified; r1 (reaction to production problems) partly verified; r9 (basis of bonuses) partly verified; r6, r7, r10 and r11 unverified. Only r2, r4 and r8 enter the main models; r3 defines a monitoring-breadth variant. r10 and r11 enter only the complementary latent analyses (Section 6.9), with a sensitivity analysis that excludes them. The practices are single binary indicators: there is no latent measurement model, and cross-country differences in reporting cannot be tested for invariance.

**Structure among the practices.** Item r8 is asked of all establishments, not only of those with targets, but it is related to r4: 64.5% of establishments with targets report target-linked bonuses against 38.1% of those without, and 14% of establishments reporting such bonuses (35% in Germany) report no targets. The correlations are moderate (φ = 0.41 for monitoring and targets, 0.22 for targets and incentives, 0.27 for monitoring and incentives), and variance inflation factors after controls are 1.05 to 1.21, so the three coefficients are separately estimable. Bonuses linked to targets without reported targets suggests that respondents may interpret the two items differently, which weakens inference on the interaction of targets and incentives. The pattern in Japan (87% with targets, 47% monitoring indicators) is atypical and may reflect differences in the understanding or translation of items rather than in practice.

**Outcomes and controls.** Outcomes are log sales per employee, annualised three-year sales growth and annualised three-year employment growth (the definitions of the base-year variables are assumed, not verified). Sales per employee is truncated at the 0.5th and 99.5th percentiles within country, and the growth outcomes at the 1st and 99th percentiles of the pooled sample; results for productivity are insensitive to no truncation, to 5/95 truncation and to median regression (Section 6.4). Sales are in local currency, so levels are compared only within country. Growth over three years is observed only for establishments that survived, and reverse causality (growing firms adopting incentives) cannot be excluded. Controls are log employment, log age and sector fixed effects (sector codes are country-specific, so sector-by-country effects absorb country levels). Organisational moderators are foreign ownership of at least 10% (b2b), exporter status (d3b, d3c), top-manager experience (b7) and female top manager (b7a); their labels were checked only against data-dictionary records of other 2024 surveys.

**Survey design.** Sampling weights exist but the module universe is a subset of the sampled establishments, and stratum and cluster identifiers are not in the file. Establishments are sampled within strata (including sector), so sector is a stratum rather than necessarily a cluster; main analyses are unweighted with HC3 errors, and sector-by-country clustering is used as a conservative sensitivity analysis (73 clusters, 7 to 15 per country). Weighted analyses are reported as robustness checks.
