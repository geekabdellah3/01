# 7. Discussion

**Universal structure, qualified.** For the six common items, practices share a dominant continuous dimension in every country, and latent classes add little. This supports universality of structure (H1) in a limited sense: congruent loadings, but not strict measurement equivalence (H2 partly supported). The common dimension is a statistical regularity among observed practices; it is not a theory of control and does not show that practices are integrated into systems.

**Effectiveness: heterogeneous but not interpretable as contextual fit.** The association between the score and productivity is positive in most countries, weak in France and the United Kingdom, and heterogeneous in most specifications (H3 moderately supported for productivity, not supported for growth). Three considerations prevent a contextual-fit interpretation. First, the countries with weak slopes combine high adoption, low reliability and, for the score, partial non-invariance; restricted range or measurement error would produce the same pattern. Second, country indicators bundle institutions, culture, sampling frames and sector composition, and seven countries cannot identify which, if any, matters. Third, fit is an alignment between a practice and a context, and neither the implementation of the practice nor the context is measured. The finding that effectiveness *may* vary across countries is thus an invitation to measure integration and context directly, not evidence that national systems require distinct control arrangements.

**Combinations and equifinality.** Configurations add little beyond the continuous score, and firm-level moderators are not reliably different from zero after correction. The productivity advantage of monitoring-plus-bonus combinations over monitoring-plus-targets, and the class-by-country interactions for growth, are exploratory leads of small magnitude. Equifinality is neither demonstrated nor rejected because the equivalence intervals are too wide.

**Accumulation, configuration, integration.** Accumulation of practices accounts for most of the structure and the performance association that can be detected; configuration adds little; integration is unobserved. This asymmetry limits what co-occurrence data can say about management control *systems*: a firm can hold indicators, targets and bonuses without those mechanisms operating as a coherent system (Grabner & Moers, 2013), and a country can display similar prevalence without similar use (Simons, 1995). Consistent with Otley (2016), a predictive search for a universally optimal configuration is not supported by these data, and neither is a claim that every context requires a unique arrangement.
