# 10. Limitations and future research

**Priority limitations.** (i) The wording and categories of the practice items could not be verified against the original questionnaires; r2 and r8 are partly verified, r4 is inferred, and r10, r11, r6 and r7 are unverified and excluded from the main models. Verification against the original questionnaires and labelled files is required before submission. (ii) The practices are single reported binary indicators; measurement invariance cannot be tested for them, and differences in reporting across countries cannot be excluded. (iii) The latent score is only partly invariant and its reliability differs across countries. (iv) Associations are cross-sectional, unweighted in the main analyses and without design-based variance estimation; sales are in local currency and the growth variables' definitions are assumed. (v) With seven countries, institutional or cultural moderators cannot be identified, and country effects are not measures of either. (vi) Predictive gains are small and the intervals from cross-validation are approximate. (vii) Hypotheses were formulated after exploratory work and not pre-registered. (viii) The literature review is a verified selection, not a systematic review of the transfer of control practices.

**Future research.** Measure integration and use of controls directly (diagnostic and interactive use, articulation among controls, managerial ownership); collect panel data around the introduction of practices; use verified institutional indicators with many more countries and multilevel designs.

**A separate research agenda on Morocco.** The G7 findings are not generalised to Moroccan organisations, and the dataset contains no Moroccan firms. The central question is under what organisational and institutional conditions internationally diffused management-control practices become effective and sustainable in Moroccan organisations (SMEs, public establishments and enterprises, family-owned businesses, subsidiaries of multinational companies). Existing Moroccan work identified through a single search includes a survey of 190 industrial firms relating contingency factors to control design (Hammouch, Manta & Palazzo, 2024) and an exploratory study of changes in control systems of large industrial companies (Daanoune & Tirhzaoui); these records were read at abstract level. Proposed studies: interviews with management accountants and controllers, organisational case studies, manager surveys with measures of control integration, longitudinal implementation studies and measures of readiness and resistance to change (Armenakis & Harris, 2002), connected to the doctoral project on management control, artificial intelligence, internal auditing and dynamic capabilities (Teece, 2007), to be examined in a distinct study.
