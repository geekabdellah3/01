# 11. Limitations and future research

**Limitations.** (i) The original country files and questionnaires were not available; wording of r6, r7, r10 and r11 and category order are unverified; outcome base-year variables and moderator labels are unverified. (ii) Measurement invariance is partial; the latent score is not strictly comparable across countries, and its reliability varies from 0.35 to 0.65. (iii) The module covers mainly firms with 20 or more employees. (iv) Analyses are cross-sectional, unweighted in the main specifications, without design-based variance estimation, and sales are in local currency. (v) With seven countries, institutional or cultural moderators cannot be tested; country effects are not measures of either. (vi) The hypotheses were formulated after exploratory work and are not pre-registered; many tests were run and conclusions rest on the pattern across specifications. (vii) A systematic literature search remains to be completed. (viii) M3 could not be estimated; institutional indicators were deliberately not added because seven countries cannot identify them. (ix) Predictive gains and transfer R² are small, and cross-validated comparisons use normal-approximation intervals that ignore dependence across folds. (x) The mapping of items to framework constructs and the coverage scores are judgemental, and item-structure tests are confounded by questionnaire blocks. **Additional analyses requiring raw files:** verification of all labels, design variables, other waves, and firm-level variables on managerial autonomy and governance.

**Future research.** (1) Measure integration and use of controls directly (diagnostic and interactive use, articulation among controls, managerial ownership). (2) Collect panel data around the introduction of controls. (3) Use country-level institutional indicators with many more countries and multilevel designs.

**A separate research agenda on Morocco.** The G7 findings are not generalised to Moroccan organisations, and the dataset contains no Moroccan firms. The agenda is motivated by the conceptual distinction between adopting, combining and integrating controls. Its central question is: under what organisational and institutional conditions can internationally diffused management-control practices become effective and sustainable in Moroccan organisations? Contexts of interest include SMEs, public establishments and public enterprises, family-owned businesses and subsidiaries of multinational companies. Existing Moroccan work identified through a single search includes a survey of 190 industrial firms relating contingency factors to control design (Hammouch, Manta & Palazzo, 2024) and an exploratory study of changes in control systems of large industrial companies (Daanoune & Tirhzaoui); these records were read at abstract level and their quality was not assessed. Proposed studies: interviews with management accountants and controllers; organisational case studies; manager surveys with measures of control integration; longitudinal implementation studies; and measures of readiness and resistance to change. This agenda connects to the doctoral project on management control systems, artificial intelligence, internal auditing and performance, where AI-enabled control, audit follow-up and dynamic capabilities (Teece, 2007) and readiness for change (Armenakis & Harris, 2002) would be examined in a distinct study.
