# Deliverable B — Revised theoretical framework

Figure: `04_FIGURES/publication_ready/Fig_Framework_contextual_fit.png`.

**Chain:** Management practices → Organisational implementation → Contextual alignment → Organisational outcomes.

| Link | Status in WBES | How it is treated |
|---|---|---|
| Practices (monitoring r2/r3/r1, targets r4–r7, bonuses r8/r9, r10/r11) | **Observed** (wording partly unverified) | Described and structured statistically |
| Structure (common dimension; weak classes) | **Estimated** | Tested (Deliverable D, earlier LCA phase) |
| Implementation/integration (use, articulation, ownership) | **Not observed** | Theoretical mechanism and future-research proposition |
| Contextual alignment ("fit") | **Not identifiable**: needs a measured context and a measured alignment | Theoretical; only firm-level moderators and country heterogeneity are testable |
| Outcomes (productivity, growth) | **Observed**, associational | Tested; no causal reading |

## Five perspectives, with what each is used for
- **Malmi & Brown (2008):** vocabulary for the five control types and for the package idea. WBES covers mainly cybernetic (monitoring, targets) and reward (bonus) controls; it does not measure the whole package. Presence of a practice is not integration into a coherent system (Grabner & Moers, 2013).
- **Simons (1995), with Tessier & Otley (2012):** effectiveness of monitoring/targets/incentives may depend on diagnostic vs interactive *use*, and on belief and boundary systems. WBES measures none of these; they appear as propositions only.
- **Bloom & Van Reenen (2007) and successors:** the universalist reading (more structured management → higher productivity). We test whether the association is stable across seven economies; Bloom, Sadun & Van Reenen (2012) show that organisational choices such as decentralisation vary with country-level trust, which motivates the contextual question (not tested here).
- **Institutional and contingency perspectives:** Otley (1980/2016) and Chenhall (2003) on contingency; Drazin & Van de Ven (1985) on forms of fit (selection, interaction, systems) and Gresov & Drazin (1997) on equifinality; Burns & Scapens (2000) on rules and routines in management accounting change; DiMaggio & Powell (1983) on isomorphism; Hall & Soskice (2001) on varieties of capitalism; Harrison & McKinnon (1999) and McSweeney (2002) on the limits of national-culture explanations. **Culture, institutions and country fixed effects are three distinct things; the data contain only the third.**
- **Nielsen (2022):** exploratory methods can reveal structure not specified in advance; LCA and continuous latent-trait models are complementary lenses; statistical structure is not theoretical interpretation, and unsupervised methods do not automatically discover meaningful organisational systems.

## Four concepts kept separate
1. **Universality of practices** — widely observed (descriptive: shares of monitoring, targets, bonuses by country).
2. **Universality of structure** — a common statistical dimension (tested: one dominant factor; partial invariance).
3. **Universality of effectiveness** — stable association with performance (tested: positive in five of seven countries for productivity (FRA, GBR CIs include zero); heterogeneity detected in most but not all specifications and entangled with measurement).
4. **Contextual fit** — effectiveness depends on alignment with context (**not tested**; theoretical).

## Management-control transfer (theoretical mechanisms only)
Budgets, indicators, targets or bonuses introduced from elsewhere may not become integrated into decisions because of limited managerial ownership, misalignment with decision processes, weak linkage to routines, incompatible incentive structures, institutional constraints, insufficient capabilities or resistance to change. None is measured in WBES, and WBES cannot show why a reform was abandoned.

## Propositions (theoretical, not findings)
P1: The performance association of formal practices depends on their integration into decision routines. P2: Integration depends on managerial ownership and compatibility with local governance and labour arrangements. P3: A common architecture of practices is compatible with heterogeneous outcomes without implying that the same arrangements are equally appropriate everywhere. P4: Equifinality (comparable performance from different combinations) is more plausible when functional demands conflict (Gresov & Drazin, 1997).
