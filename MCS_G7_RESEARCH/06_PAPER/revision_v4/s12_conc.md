# 12. Conclusion

Read through general management frameworks, the evidence is partly consistent with a universal management technology (common structure, mostly positive productivity associations), not supported by measured firm-level contingencies, and consistent with — but not distinguishable from artefacts of — institutional decoupling at high adoption. Judged by out-of-sample prediction, a universal model of the practice–performance association is not improved on by organisational contingency or by national slopes, and models transfer imperfectly between single countries but well from a pool of countries. Formalising the control frameworks shows that WBES observes only cybernetic, results and reward controls, which behave as one dimension. Related to the management control literature, observed practices describe an accumulation of cybernetic and reward controls whose integration and use are unobserved. Whether and why contextual fit matters requires direct measurement of integration, use and context. The article contributes a bounded empirical result, an explicit mapping from general to control concepts and a research agenda, not an established theory of contextual fit.
