# Critical literature synthesis (verification status explicit)

**Scope and honesty note.** Every bibliographic record below was checked against search-engine records of publisher, repository or RePEc pages during this project. For most items only the abstract/metadata was visible; **no full text was read**. Statements are therefore limited to what abstracts say. Items marked *(brief)* come from the research brief and were not re-verified. No DOI is given unless it appeared in a retrieved record. A systematic literature search of accounting databases (Scopus/WoS/EBSCO) is **NOT TESTED** and must be completed before any novelty claim.

## 1. Management control as a package (Malmi & Brown, 2008)
Malmi & Brown (2008, *Management Accounting Research* 19(4), 287–300, doi:10.1016/j.mar.2008.09.003) is an editorial that, per its abstract, notes the scarcity of explicit research on MCS as a package and proposes a typology of planning, cybernetic, reward and compensation, administrative and cultural controls. It is conceptual. Grabner & Moers (2013, *AOS* 38(6), 407–419, doi:10.1016/j.aos.2013.09.002) clarify the "system vs package" distinction and define internal consistency (abstract). **Our reading:** the package perspective motivates studying controls jointly but does not prescribe how combinations are to be identified empirically.

## 2. Closest competing studies on configurations
- **Bedford & Malmi (2015, *MAR* 27, 2–26, doi:10.1016/j.mar.2015.04.002)** — closest precedent: an exploratory analysis of control configurations in ~400 firms yielding five configurations (simple, results, action, devolved, hybrid) (abstract). It used a survey of top managers and cluster analysis (as indicated by the "exploratory analysis" description; the exact algorithm was not verified). Our study therefore **cannot claim that configurations have not been studied**. It differs by: other data (WBES, 7 economies, practice-level items), likelihood-based latent class estimation with explicit model comparison against continuous alternatives, and an explicit test of gradient versus configuration.
- **Bedford, Malmi & Sandelin (2016, *AOS* 51, 12–28, doi:10.1016/j.aos.2016.04.002)** — packages/systems and effectiveness (title and metadata only).
- **Murrell, Karalashvili & Francis (2024, *J. Law, Economics & Organization* 40(3), 891–929)**, preceded by the 2018 World Bank working paper 8564 (Francis, Karalashvili & Murrell) — applies LCA to Enterprise Surveys data on **governance of transactions between firms**, a different object from internal management control. It shows LCA on WBES-type data is established practice; it is not evidence about management control.
- **Hyland, Francis & Rodriguez Meza (World Bank PRWP 8783)** — uses the WBES management module in South America with aggregate measures (per title/summary); not read.
- An extended search found **no** empirical latent-class study of management-control configurations; this is a result of one search only and is not proof of absence.

## 3. Levers of control (Simons, 1995) and its measurement
Simons (1995) *(brief)* distinguishes belief, boundary, diagnostic and interactive systems; the distinction concerns **use**. Tessier & Otley (2012, *MAR*, 171–185; repository lists issue 23(3)) analyse conceptual ambiguities and propose separating managerial intentions from employee perceptions (abstract). Widener (2007, *AOS* 32(7–8), 757–788) is an empirical analysis of the framework (content not read). **Implication:** WBES items record whether practices exist, not how they are used; classes or dimensions must not be named "diagnostic" or "interactive".

## 4. Measurement of management practices (Bloom & Van Reenen, 2007)
Bloom & Van Reenen (2007, *QJE* 122(4), 1351–1408, doi:10.1162/qjec.2007.122.4.1351): practices measured in 732 medium-sized manufacturing firms in four countries are associated with productivity, profitability, Tobin's Q and survival; the US is better managed on average; there is a long tail of poorly managed firms (abstract). Bloom et al. (2019, *AER* 109(5), 1648–1683, doi:10.1257/aer.20170491) document large dispersion in US plants. Scur et al. (2021, *Oxford Rev. Econ. Policy* 37(2), 231–258, doi:10.1093/oxrep/grab009) review the WMS. The WBES module is described in World Bank briefs as grouped into monitoring, target setting and incentives. **Open question we test:** whether an aggregate score hides combinations.

## 5. Complementarity versus co-occurrence
Milgrom & Roberts (1995, *J. Accounting & Economics* 19(2–3), 179–208) formalise complementarities (supermodularity); Athey & Stern (1998, NBER WP 6600) caution that cross-sectional tests of complementarity can be biased. **Consequence:** pattern frequencies (co-occurrence) and non-additivity tests (statistical interaction) do not establish economic complementarity.

## 6. Digitalisation, exploratory analytics, change and capabilities (thesis context only)
Knudsen (2020, *Int. J. Accounting Information Systems* 36, 100441, doi:10.1016/j.accinf.2019.100441): systematic review 2007–2017 pointing to elusive accounting boundaries, power relations, knowledge production. Bhimani (2020, *J. Management Control* 31(1–2), 9–23, doi:10.1007/s00187-020-00295-z): digital data call for rethinking research methods. Nielsen (2022, *J. Accounting & Organizational Change* 18(5), 811–853): exploratory data analysis and unsupervised ML should be studied in management accounting. Teece (2007, *SMJ* 28(13), 1319–1350, doi:10.1002/smj.640) and Armenakis & Harris (2002, *JOCM* 15(2), 169–183, doi:10.1108/09534810210423080) are theoretical resources for the doctoral model; **they are not tested here**.

## 7. Methodological references
Nylund, Asparouhov & Muthén (2007, *Structural Equation Modeling* 14(4), 535–569): BIC and the bootstrap LRT perform best among class-enumeration criteria in their simulations (abstract). Vermunt (2010, *Political Analysis* 18(4), 450–469): three-step approaches and bias from classification error — motivates our use of posterior-probability and pseudo-class treatments of uncertainty (the BCH/ML three-step correction itself was **not** implemented).

## 8. Gap, stated cautiously
Not "no study has examined configurations". The defensible gap is narrower: (i) configurational evidence on **observable practice-level WBES items** across several advanced economies is, to our search, not documented; (ii) the **gradient-versus-configuration** question is rarely tested explicitly against continuous latent-trait alternatives; (iii) Simons-type constructs cannot be measured with WBES, so conclusions must remain at the level of practices.
