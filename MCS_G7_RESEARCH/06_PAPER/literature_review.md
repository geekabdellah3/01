# 2. Theoretical background and prior research

*Verification note: references were checked against bibliographic records; full texts were not read. See `05_THEORY/literature_review/CRITICAL_LITERATURE_SYNTHESIS.md` for details and the verification status of each citation.*

**Package perspective.** Malmi and Brown (2008) propose a typology of planning, cybernetic, reward and compensation, administrative and cultural controls and call for research on controls as a package. Grabner and Moers (2013) distinguish packages from designed systems and define internal consistency.

**Configurations.** Bedford and Malmi (2015) derive five configurations of control from a survey of about 400 firms. This is our closest precedent and a reason for caution about claims of novelty.

**Use of controls.** Simons (1995) differentiates belief, boundary, diagnostic and interactive systems by the way managers use them. Tessier and Otley (2012) show conceptual ambiguity and propose distinguishing managerial intentions from employee perceptions. Because use cannot be inferred from the presence of a practice, we use Simons only for interpretation.

**Measurement of management.** Bloom and Van Reenen (2007) relate scored practices to productivity, profitability and survival; Bloom et al. (2019) document large dispersion in US plants; Scur et al. (2021) review the World Management Survey. The WBES module follows this tradition and has been grouped into monitoring, target setting and incentives in World Bank briefs.

**Complementarity.** Milgrom and Roberts (1995) define complementarity through supermodularity; Athey and Stern (1998) warn that cross-sectional tests can be biased. We therefore distinguish co-occurrence, statistical interaction and economic complementarity.

**Methods.** Class enumeration follows Nylund, Asparouhov and Muthén (2007); classification uncertainty is handled with soft assignment and pseudo-class draws, motivated by Vermunt (2010).
