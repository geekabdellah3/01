# 10. Limitations and future research

**Priority limitations.** (i) The wording and categories of the practice items could not be verified against the original questionnaires; r2 and r8 are partly verified and r4 is inferred, and the author has not read the questionnaires. Verification against the original questionnaires and labelled files is required before submission, including the relation between r4 and r8 and the atypical profile of Japan. (ii) The practices are single reported binary indicators; measurement invariance cannot be tested for them, and differences in reporting across countries cannot be excluded. (iii) The comparisons have limited power: with an incremental R² of about 2%, the corrected intervals cannot exclude gains of the size predicted by the heterogeneity, and equivalence is not established. (iv) Inference on heterogeneity depends on the treatment of the design: HC3 and weighted analyses differ, and the wild cluster bootstrap with 7 to 15 clusters per country is conservative. (v) Associations are cross-sectional and unweighted in the main analyses; growth outcomes condition on survival and may reflect reverse causality; sales are in local currency and the growth variables' definitions are assumed. (vi) With seven countries, institutional or cultural moderators cannot be identified, and country effects are not measures of either. (vii) Hypotheses were formulated after exploratory work and not pre-registered. (viii) The literature review is a verified selection, not a systematic review of the transfer of control practices, and the bibliographic records were checked without reading the full texts.

**Future research.** Measure integration and use of controls directly (diagnostic and interactive use, articulation among controls, managerial ownership); collect panel data around the introduction of practices; use verified institutional indicators with many more countries and multilevel designs. Applications to other contexts, to artificial intelligence and internal audit, and to organisational readiness for change are outside the scope of this paper.
