# 8. Contributions

1. **Practice-level rather than score-level evidence.** Treating monitoring, targets and incentives separately shows heterogeneity that a single score conceals: it concerns incentives and productivity and is weak for the other practices.
2. **Heterogeneity versus predictive value.** Detectable cross-country heterogeneity in one practice does not improve out-of-sample prediction, and the paper quantifies why (oracle gain versus estimation cost), which clarifies how heterogeneity tests and predictive tests answer different questions.
3. **Conditional transferability.** Models estimated in one country transfer imperfectly, and models estimated on other countries transfer nearly as well as local ones.
4. **Bridge to management control.** The practices are interpreted as observable cybernetic, results and reward mechanisms, and the paper states which control constructs (integration, use, consistency, administrative and cultural controls) presence-based data cannot address.
