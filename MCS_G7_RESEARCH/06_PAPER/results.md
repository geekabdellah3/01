# 4. Results

**4.1 Sample and variables.** The module universe contains 6,316 establishments (CAN 580, FRA 451, DEU 1,233, ITA 641, JPN 1,321, GBR 610, USA 1,480). Cross-country heterogeneity in response distributions is substantial (Cramér's V 0.12–0.33). Shares reporting indicator monitoring (r2) range from 47% (JPN) to 92% (GBR); targets (r4) from 60% (ITA) to 93% (GBR); target-linked bonuses (r8) from 36% (ITA) to 76% (GBR). Pairwise associations among practices are modest (maximum bias-corrected V = 0.41, r2–r4).

**4.2 Number of classes.** In specification A BIC is minimal at K = 4 (53,186.6) but differs from K = 3 and K = 5 by ≤ 13 points; relative entropy is 0.53 (K = 3) and 0.60 (K = 4). Specifications B, C and D select K = 5, 4 and 6 respectively. Local independence is violated for a large share of item pairs.

**4.3 Profiles.** With K = 3 (prevalence 21%, 32%, 47%) the classes are ordered by the presence of monitoring, targets and bonuses (P(r2 = yes) = .19, .66, .95; P(r4 = yes) = .23, .92, .94; P(r8 = yes) = .27, .52, .78): no crossing occurs on these indicators (0/9). With K = 4 a small class (about 5–7%) combines near-universal targets with few bonuses; it is unstable across subsamples.

**4.4 Gradient versus configuration.** On the same likelihood a hybrid (three classes × one trait) outperforms the best LCA by 129 BIC points in specification A, and a two-dimensional continuous model outperforms LCA by 236–368 points in B, C and D. Parallel analysis retains a single factor for the six universal items. In outcome models, a continuous score and the transparent core score account for the associations; latent classes add nothing once the continuous score is included (pseudo-class F tests p between 0.29 and 0.62 across outcomes), and out-of-sample RMSE is lowest for the score models.

**4.5 Cross-country validity.** Country-specific analyses favour two classes in five countries and three in GBR and ITA. Multi-group results depend on K (invariance rejected at K = 3, retained at K = 4 by BIC). Tucker congruence of one-factor solutions is .93–.99.

**4.6 Outcomes and nonlinearity.** One SD of the continuous score is associated with +0.128 log sales per employee (HC3 SE 0.005), +0.0033 annual sales growth and +0.0018 annual employment growth. After Holm correction, two nonlinearity tests for productivity remain significant but explain ≤0.04% of variance; no interaction among monitoring, targets and bonuses survives correction.
