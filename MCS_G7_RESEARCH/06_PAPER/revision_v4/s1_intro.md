# 1. Introduction

Whether a management practice works the same way wherever it is adopted is a core question of general management research. Universalist accounts treat management as a technology whose adoption raises productivity (Bloom & Van Reenen, 2007; Bloom, Sadun & Van Reenen, 2016). Contingency theory holds that there is no single best way and that performance depends on fit with conditions (Lawrence & Lorsch, 1967; Donaldson, 2001). Institutional theory adds that practices may be adopted for legitimacy and remain decoupled from operations (Meyer & Rowan, 1977; Zbaracki, 1998), and work on the transfer of practices shows that success depends on institutionalisation in the receiving unit and on its institutional and organisational context (Kostova, 1999).

This article interprets evidence on observable management practices through these general frameworks and then relates it to the management control literature. We use the management-practices module of the World Bank Enterprise Surveys (WBES) for seven G7 economies in 2024–2025 (6,316 establishments) and ask: to what extent are the structure and the performance associations of observable management practices universal across advanced economies, and how far do models of those associations transfer from one national context to another?

Three propositions organise the study. **P1 (structural universality):** some control practices share a common architecture across countries. **P2 (functional contextualisation):** their association with performance may depend on organisational and institutional characteristics. **P3 (conditional transferability):** a model estimated in one national context may lose predictive power in another. Three competing models operationalise them: a universal model (M1), an organisational-contingency model (M2) and a nationally contextualised model (M3). We judge them by out-of-sample prediction, not only by coefficients, and we first formalise the main management control frameworks and map them to the WBES items to establish what the data can address at all. Management control concepts (Malmi & Brown, 2008; Grabner & Moers, 2013; Simons, 1995) enter as domain-specific counterparts of the general concepts of adoption, integration and use.

Four ideas are kept apart: universality of *practices*, of *structure*, of *effectiveness* and *contextual fit*. Only the first three can be examined with WBES; contextual fit is a theoretical interpretation. The paper preserves the findings of an earlier exploratory phase, in which practices were organised predominantly along a continuous dimension rather than in distinct latent classes.
