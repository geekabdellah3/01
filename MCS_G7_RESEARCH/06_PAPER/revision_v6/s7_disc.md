# 7. Discussion

## 7.1 Answer to the central question
The three practices do not show the same associations with productivity: incentives are most strongly associated, monitoring less so, targets not at all. The association of incentives appears to vary strongly across the seven countries, but this heterogeneity is statistically fragile (it weakens with weights and with wild cluster inference), and no heterogeneity was detected for monitoring or targets with productivity. No evidence of joint effects and no reliable organisational contingencies were found. A common model of the three practices is not detectably improved on out of sample by country-specific or contingent slopes, a partial-pooling model gives the best point estimate, and a model estimated on the other six countries is close to, but slightly worse than, a local one. The common model remains the predictive benchmark, but the comparison has limited power and cannot establish equivalence.

## 7.2 Interpretation through management control concepts
Read as control mechanisms, the results concern reward control most: the estimated association of incentives is the strongest and the most country-dependent, which is compatible with the idea that rewards depend on how they are designed and used locally, a point on which the data are silent. The association of monitoring (cybernetic and diagnostic control) is more homogeneous and present in three countries, and targets (planning and results control) show little association with productivity. The absence of detectable interactions means that presence indicators do not reveal combined effects, although the overlap between the targets and incentives items limits this inference. Whether practices form a coherent system (Grabner & Moers, 2013) or are used diagnostically or interactively (Simons, 1995) cannot be assessed, so the contribution to the control literature is a precise statement of what presence-based data can and cannot show.

## 7.3 Interpretation through general management frameworks
The universal-technology reading is supported for incentives and monitoring in some countries (incentives in five of seven, monitoring in three) but not for targets and not uniformly. The contingency reading is not supported with the organisational contingencies available. The institutional and transfer reading is consistent with suggestive heterogeneity for incentives and with imperfect single-country transfer, but the data cannot separate institutional explanations from composition, measurement error and ceiling effects, and country effects are not measures of culture or institutions. Decoupling and ceremonial adoption would require measures of integration and use.

## 7.4 What cannot be concluded
That each country needs its own control model, or that contexts are irrelevant, is not established; neither is it established that a pooled model is equivalent to a local one. The associations are small (maximum local R² of 0.08), practices are single reported indicators of partly unverified wording, and integration, use and institutional characteristics are not measured.
