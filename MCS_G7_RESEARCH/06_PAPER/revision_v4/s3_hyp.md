# 3. Propositions, competing models and hypotheses

**Central question.** To what extent are the structure and performance associations of management practices universal across advanced economies, and how far do models of those associations transfer across national and organisational contexts?

**Propositions.** P1 (structural universality): some control practices have a common architecture across countries. P2 (functional contextualisation): the association with performance may depend on organisational and institutional characteristics. P3 (conditional transferability): a model estimated in one national context may lose predictive power elsewhere.

**Competing models.** M1 (universal): Performance_ic = α_c + β·MCS_ic + γ′X_ic + ε, with a single β for all countries (Bloom & Van Reenen). M2 (organisational contingency): M1 plus MCS × organisational characteristics (Chenhall; Otley). M3 (national contextualisation): M2 plus MCS × Z_c, with Z_c national institutional characteristics (institutional theory; Kostova). M3 cannot be estimated with seven countries, and it is nested in a model with country-specific slopes; the national-slope model therefore serves as the upper bound for any Z_c interaction.

**Operational hypotheses** (derived from the frameworks of Section 2; formulated after an earlier exploratory phase on the same data; not pre-registered).
- **H1 (P1; technology).** The practices share a common dimension across countries. *Factor structure, discrete-versus-continuous comparison, country-specific solutions.*
- **H2 (P1; measurement).** The measurement structure differs across countries. *Multi-group invariance, item differential functioning.*
- **H3 (P2; technology versus contingency).** The score–performance association varies across countries. *Country-specific slopes, formal equality tests.*
- **H4 (P2; contingency and capabilities).** The association depends on organisational characteristics and combinations of practices. *Moderators, equal-score combinations, class-by-country interactions.*
- **H5 (P2; decoupling versus artefact).** The association is weaker where adoption is high and flattens at high levels of the score. *Level-dependent shapes, common support.*
- **H6 (equifinality).** Combinations with equal core scores perform comparably.
- **H7 (framework structure).** WBES items follow the structure implied by control frameworks (cybernetic versus reward, monitoring versus targets versus rewards). *Confirmatory item-structure comparison.*
- **H8 (P2, P3; prediction and transfer).** National slopes or organisational contingency improve out-of-sample prediction over M1, and models transfer imperfectly across countries. *Cross-validated comparison; transferability matrix.*
