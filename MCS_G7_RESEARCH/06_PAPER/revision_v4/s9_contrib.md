# 9. Theoretical contributions

1. **Interpretation before domain translation.** Each general framework (technology, contingency, institutional decoupling, transfer, capabilities, equifinality) is translated into an observable implication and compared with the evidence (Table 13). The comparison shows which frameworks the data can and cannot discriminate: the technology reading is partly supported, the contingency reading is not supported with the contingencies available, and institutional decoupling is consistent but not distinguishable from ceiling and measurement artefacts.
2. **Competing models and transferability.** Cross-validated comparison of universal, contingent and national models, with an international transferability matrix, shows that the universal model is not improved on out of sample by organisational contingency or national slopes, and that single-country models transfer imperfectly but pooled models transfer well. The nesting of any institutional interaction within national slopes makes explicit what a seven-country sample can and cannot support.
3. **Measurement and heterogeneity.** Observable practices have a largely common continuous structure across seven advanced economies, with partial, K-dependent invariance and differential reliability; the association with productivity is positive in most countries, heterogeneous in most specifications and partly level-dependent. Cross-country comparison therefore requires explicit invariance checks and measurement-error treatment.
4. **Formalisation and rapprochement with management control.** Table 14 positions the package, system, use, institutionalisation, contingency and configuration concepts of the control literature as domain-specific counterparts of adoption, integration, implementation, fit and equifinality. The distinction between adopting, combining and integrating controls follows from that mapping. The proposition that a common architecture of practices can coexist with heterogeneous organisational outcomes, without implying that identical arrangements are equally appropriate everywhere, is **a theoretical interpretation consistent with the evidence; it is not demonstrated contextual fit.**
