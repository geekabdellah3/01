# 5. Empirical strategy

**Individual associations (Question 1).** Performance_ic = α_s(i) + β_M·M_ic + β_T·T_ic + β_I·I_ic + γ′X_ic + ε_ic, where s(i) is the sector-by-country fixed effect and X contains log size and log age (HC3 standard errors); the three practices enter together so that coefficients are partial associations. No causal interpretation is made.

**Combinations (Question 2).** The specification adds M×T, M×I and T×I (and a three-way term), tested jointly and individually, with Holm correction within the family. A statistical interaction is not complementarity in the strong sense.

**Contexts (Question 3).** Country-specific coefficients are estimated separately by country and, in a pooled model, by interacting each practice with country (with country-specific control slopes). Equality of each practice's coefficients across the seven countries is tested with robust Wald statistics (6 df; HC3 and sector-clustered), and heterogeneity is summarised with Cochran's Q and I² (Higgins & Thompson, 2002) and a random-effects mean; Holm's correction (Holm, 1979) is applied across the nine practice-by-outcome tests. Organisational moderators are tested by interacting the three practices with each moderator (joint 3 df) with Holm correction over 18 tests.

**Prediction and transfer.** Models are compared by 10-fold cross-validation repeated three times, with folds stratified by country and identical across models. Every model carries the same controls and main effects of the moderators, so that differences isolate the practice terms: U0 (controls only), P1 (common slopes for M, T, I), P2 (P1 plus combinations), C1 (P1 plus practices × organisational moderators), N1 (country-specific slopes for each practice) and X (C1 plus country-specific slopes). N1 bounds any model with a national institutional interaction. Paired differences in squared error are reported with a firm-level interval, which ignores dependence across folds, and with the corrected resampled interval of Nadeau and Bengio (2003) computed on fold-level errors. Transferability is assessed on country-by-sector-demeaned data: a model estimated on one source country, or on the other six countries (LOCO), is applied to each target and compared with a model estimated and cross-validated in the target, and with a global pooled model that adds the target's training folds; paired bootstrap intervals are reported for the differences.

**Why a common model can stay competitive.** The oracle gain of knowing country slopes exactly (the estimated slope heterogeneity multiplied by the residual variance of each practice) is compared with the out-of-sample cost of estimating the additional slopes, and the out-of-sample power of national slopes is simulated at the estimated heterogeneity and at multiples of it.

**Complementary analyses.** Latent structure (one-dimensional and class models), cross-country invariance, level dependence and a latent-score sensitivity analysis that excludes items of unverified wording are reported in Section 6.9.
