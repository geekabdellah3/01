# 7. Discussion

## 7.1 Answer to the central question
The three practices do not show the same associations across countries. Their individual associations with productivity differ in strength (incentives > monitoring > targets), and the association of incentives differs strongly across the seven countries, while that of monitoring does not. No evidence was found that the practices operate jointly beyond their individual associations, and organisational contingencies do not moderate the associations reliably. Nevertheless, a common model of the three practices predicts out of sample as well as models with country-specific slopes, and a model estimated on the other six countries transfers nearly as well as a local one. Structure is common, associations are partly practice- and country-specific, and the common model remains the predictive benchmark: statistically detectable heterogeneity does not translate into predictive gains at the size observed (Section 6.7).

## 7.2 Interpretation through management control concepts
Read as control mechanisms, the results concern reward control most: the association of incentives is the strongest and the most country-dependent, which is compatible with the idea that rewards depend on how they are designed and used locally, a point on which the data are silent. The association of monitoring (cybernetic and diagnostic control) is more homogeneous, and targets (planning and results control) show little association with productivity. The absence of interactions among practices means that presence indicators do not reveal combined effects; whether practices form a coherent system (Grabner & Moers, 2013) or are used diagnostically or interactively (Simons, 1995) cannot be assessed, so the contribution to the control literature is a precise statement of what presence-based data can and cannot show.

## 7.3 Interpretation through general management frameworks
The universal-technology reading is supported for monitoring and incentives in most countries but not for incentives uniformly. The contingency reading is not supported with the organisational contingencies available. The institutional and transfer reading is consistent with practice-specific heterogeneity and with imperfect single-country transfer, but the data cannot separate institutional explanations from composition, measurement error and ceiling effects, and country effects are not measures of culture or institutions. Decoupling and ceremonial adoption would require measures of integration and use.

## 7.4 What cannot be concluded
That each country needs its own control model, or that contexts are irrelevant, is not established. The associations are small (maximum local R² of 0.08), practices are single reported indicators of partly unverified wording, and integration, use and institutional characteristics are not measured.
