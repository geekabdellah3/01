# 6. Empirical strategy

**Structure and measurement (retained and extended).** Categorical latent class models (one to eight classes) were compared on the same likelihood with a one-dimensional nominal-response trait model, a two-dimensional model and a hybrid (classes × one trait), using BIC, entropy and posterior probabilities. Cross-country comparability was assessed with multi-group latent class models (item-response probabilities shared versus free), item-wise likelihood-ratio tests of differential functioning and country-specific solutions. A five-item score excluding r8 (the item with differential functioning in both the three- and four-class solutions) serves as a sensitivity measure.

**Country-specific associations.** For each country c, y_i = α_s(i) + β_c θ_i + γ_c′X_i + ε_i, where s(i) is the sector fixed effect and X contains log size and log age. The latent trait θ is the posterior mean of the nominal one-dimensional model computed with a country-specific prior (regression calibration), which accounts for differential reliability across countries; the pooled-prior posterior mean, the five-item score and the core score (number of practices present among r2, r4, r8) are alternatives. Unconditional plausible values were tried first and rejected because they attenuate slopes by roughly the reliability.

**Heterogeneity.** The pooled model y_ic = α_s(i) + Σ_c β_c θ_i·1[c] + γ′X_i + ε is used to test H0: β_1 = … = β_7 with robust Wald statistics (HC3 and sector-clustered), with and without country-specific control slopes. Heterogeneity in separately estimated slopes is summarised by Cochran's Q, I² (Higgins & Thompson, 2002), a DerSimonian–Laird random-effects mean and a 95% prediction interval. Because results depend on scoring and specification, results are reported as a specification summary rather than a single test.

**Contingencies and equifinality.** Interactions of θ with size, age, foreign ownership, export status, manager experience and manager gender, with and without θ-by-country terms. Equifinality: equality of adjusted performance across combinations of r2, r4, r8 with the same core score (levels one and two), with equivalence judged by confidence intervals against explicit, illustrative margins (±0.05 log points; ±0.005 for growth). Class-by-country interactions and country-specific nonlinearity (spline versus linear) complete the analysis. Holm corrections are applied within families (Holm, 1979). All results are associations; no causal claim is made.

**Item structures implied by control frameworks.** Seven items with verified or partly verified wording (r1, r2, r3, r4, r5, r8, r9) were assigned to the factors of each framework-implied structure and compared on the same likelihood with nominal-response models: one common factor; cybernetic {r1–r5} versus reward {r8, r9} (Malmi and Brown-type); monitoring {r1–r3} versus results controls {r4, r5, r8, r9} (Merchant and Van der Stede-type); three families (monitoring, targets, rewards; Otley-type); and an unrestricted two-factor model. Factors are exchangeably correlated (grid 0.5–0.95). Gate items and follow-up items share questionnaire blocks, which can mimic family structure.

**Competing predictive models and transferability.** Models U0 (controls), M1 (common linear slope), M1′ (common level-dependent shape), M2 (M1′ plus score × organisational characteristics), national-slope models and their combination are compared by 10-fold cross-validation repeated three times, with folds stratified by country and identical for all models. All models carry the same controls and the same main effects of the moderators so that differences isolate the control-practice terms. The item parameters of the score are re-estimated within training folds and applied to test firms; the score does not use outcomes. Paired comparisons use firm-level squared errors. M3 is not estimated (Section 3). Transferability is assessed with slope-only models on country-by-sector-demeaned data: a model estimated on a source country (or on the other six countries, LOCO) is applied to each target country, and its out-of-sample R² is compared with that of a model estimated locally by repeated cross-validation; bootstrap intervals (200 replicates) are reported for transferred models.
