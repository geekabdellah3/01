# 8. Theoretical contributions

1. **Measurement.** Observable practices have a largely common continuous structure across seven advanced economies, with partial, K-dependent invariance and differential reliability; cross-country comparison requires explicit invariance checks and measurement-error treatment.
2. **Contextual heterogeneity.** The association between practice sophistication and productivity is positive in most countries but heterogeneous in most specifications; its heterogeneity cannot be attributed to national institutions or culture with these data, and firm-level moderators are not reliably different from zero.
3. **Concepts.** Separating universality of practices, structure and effectiveness from contextual fit, and accumulation and configuration from integration, clarifies what presence-based data can support. The proposition that a common architecture of practices can coexist with heterogeneous organisational outcomes, without implying that identical arrangements are equally appropriate everywhere, is **a theoretical interpretation consistent with the evidence on structure and with moderate evidence of heterogeneous associations; it is not demonstrated contextual fit.**
